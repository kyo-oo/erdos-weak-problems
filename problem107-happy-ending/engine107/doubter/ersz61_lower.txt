2. THE CUP/CAP EXTREMAL SETS (Theorem 2.5 of [MS00], verbatim) -- the parts
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
"Theorem 2.5 ([32]).  f(k,l) = C(k+l-4, k-2) + 1.

Proof. Note that we have already observed this to be the case when k or l is 3.
We proceed by induction. Suppose that we have a set A of C(k+l-5, k-3) points
with no (k-1)-cup and no l-cap, and a set B of C(k+l-5, k-2) points with no
k-cup and no (l-1)-cap. Translate these sets so that the following conditions
are satisfied:
   (i) every point of B has greater first coordinate than the first
       coordinates of points of A,
   (ii) the slope of any line connecting a point of A to a point of B is
       greater than the slope of any line connecting two points of A or two
       points of B.
Let X = A U B be the resulting configuration. Any cup in X that contains
elements of both A and B may have only one element of B. It follows that X
contains no k-cup. We similarly see that X contains no l-cap. Thus
   f(k,l) >= C(k+l-5, k-3) + C(k+l-5, k-2) + 1 = C(k+l-4, k-2) + 1."

[Note the extremal object: a set E(k,l) of exactly C(k+l-4, k-2) points with
 NO k-cup and NO l-cap, built recursively: E(k,l) = A U B with A a translated
 copy of E(k-1,l) to the LOWER LEFT (smaller x's), B a copy of E(k,l-1) to the
 UPPER RIGHT, all A->B line slopes STRICTLY GREATER than every slope of a line
 through two points of A or two points of B. Base: E(2,l) and E(k,2) = one
 point. |E(k,l)| = C(k+l-4, k-2) by Pascal. Why cups/caps die: a mixed cup
 (slopes strictly increasing along increasing x) can contain at most one
 element of B, since after entering B via an A->B segment (slope larger than
 all internal slopes) any following B-internal segment has smaller slope --
 contradicting monotone increase; hence mixed cup = (cup in A of length <= k-2)
 + at most 1 point of B, total <= k-1. Symmetrically a mixed cap contains at
 most one element of A, total <= 1 + (l-2) = l-1.]

3. THE 2^{n-2} CONSTRUCTION (Theorem 2.6 of [MS00], verbatim) -- the whole
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
"Theorem 2.6 ([33],[51]).  N(n) >= 2^{n-2} + 1.

Proof. To prove the inequality, we construct a set X of 2^{n-2} points with no
subset of n points in convex position. For i = 0, 1, ..., n-2, let T_i be a set
of C(n-2, i) points containing no (i+2)-cap and no (n-i)-cup and having the
property that no two points in the set are connected by a line having slope of
absolute value greater than 1.

For i = 0, 1, ..., n-2, place a small copy of T_i in a neighborhood of the
point on the unit circle making an angle of pi/4 - i*pi/(2(n-2)) with the
positive x-axis. Let X be the union of the T_i, i = 0, 1, ..., n-2. Then
        |X| = sum_{i=0}^{n-2} C(n-2, i) = 2^{n-2}.

Suppose that Y is a subset of X in convex position. Let k and l be the smallest
and the largest values of i so that Y INTERSECT T_i != empty. If k = l, then Y
contains no (k+2)-cap and no (n-k)-cup. The construction guarantees that:
   (a) Y INTERSECT T_k is a cap of at most k+1 points,
   (b) Y INTERSECT T_l is a cup of at most n-l-1 points,
   (c) |Y INTERSECT T_i| <= 1 for all i = k+1, k+2, ..., l-1.
Thus
   |Y| <= k+1 + l-k-1 + n-l-1 = n-1.
Hence no subset of X in convex position contains n points."

WHY IT WORKS (W67-107D structural analysis, exact slope separation):
  * T_i = E(n-i, i+2) from Theorem 2.5's extremal construction: it has exactly
    C(n-i+i+2-4, n-i-2) = C(n-2, n-i-2) = C(n-2, i) points, no (n-i)-CUP and
    no (i+2)-CAP. The E-construction can be realized with ALL pairwise line
    slopes in the open interval (0,1): compose children flattened so their
    internal slopes lie in (0, 1/3), and place B so that every A->B line slope
    lies in (1/2, 1) -- strictly above all internal slopes, satisfying
    Theorem 2.5 condition (ii), while every pairwise slope of the union stays
    < 1 in absolute value, satisfying the T_i slope condition.
  * The clusters T_i sit on the arc of the unit circle from angle +pi/4 (i=0,
    TOP-RIGHT) down to -pi/4 (i=n-2, BOTTOM-RIGHT), passing through angle 0
    (i=(n-2)/2, RIGHTMOST). The chord between two circle points at angles
    a > b has slope -cot((a+b)/2), hence EVERY line through points of two
    different clusters has slope of magnitude >= 1 (up to the tiny "small copy"
    perturbation), while every line through two points of one cluster has all
    |slope| <= 1 (indeed in (0,1)). INTER-cluster lines are steep, INTRA-
    cluster lines are shallow: this dichotomy is what forces (a),(b),(c):
    a convex polygon's boundary can pass through a middle cluster only in a
    single point (its entering/exiting edges are steep, its internal edges
    shallow, breaking the monotone turning of the convex boundary), while at
    the topmost used cluster k the polygon's boundary chain is a CAP (bounded
    below by a single edge; caps in T_k have <= k+1 points since T_k has no
    (k+2)-cap) and at the bottommost used cluster l it is a CUP (<= n-l-1
    points since T_l has no (n-i)-cup).
  * SIZES: |X| = 2^{n-2}: n=3 -> 2 points, n=4 -> 4 (triangle+interior), n=5
    -> 8 (the Wikipedia "eight points, no convex pentagon" figure), n=6 -> 16
    (the Wikipedia "sixteen points, no convex hexagon" figure), n=7 -> 32.

COROLLARY (the statement under attack): f(n) >= 2^{n-2} + 1 for all n >= 3,
and the Erdos-Szekeres conjecture f(n) = 2^{n-2}+1 is OPEN for n >= 7
